# Lessons 4–6 built on request, ahead of demonstrated need

The user asked to fill in the locked placeholder cards across the hub, so lessons 4–6 of Emerging AI Research now exist. This is a coverage event, not a learning event: the lessons were written (each by one agent, then independently reviewed for accuracy, citations and quiz correctness) but not yet worked through, so nothing here records demonstrated understanding.

**What each lesson is for** (the single tangible win it was designed around):

- [[../lessons/0004-mechanistic-interpretability-circuits.html|Lesson 4]] — Given any interpretability headline ('researchers found the X feature / the Y circuit in model Z'), the learner can name the method behind it (probe, activation patching or ablation, SAE feature with top-activating examples, steering, attribution graph).…
- [[../lessons/0005-scaling-laws-established-vs-debated.html|Lesson 5]] — Given any scaling claim, the learner can sort it into one of three kinds: the established empirical result (pretraining cross-entropy loss falls as a power law in parameters, data and compute, within the measured range); a revised or contested prescription…
- [[../lessons/0006-evaluating-new-architecture-claims.html|Lesson 6]] — Given a new architecture headline and its paper, the learner runs a fixed claim audit: triage the claim type, check the matched comparison (compute, data, params AND tuning effort), check the scale regime, set a base-rate prior, then follow the evidence…

**What to watch for next time.** Treat these as unverified until the user engages with them. The first follow-up question, or a quiz they report getting wrong, is the real signal for the zone of proximal development; until then, don't assume the material in these lessons is known when planning further work.
