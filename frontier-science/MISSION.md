# Mission: Frontier Science

## Why
Direct sibling to [[../quantum-mechanics/MISSION.md|Quantum Mechanics]] — same "modern, rigorous, not the kind of thing most people actually understand" instinct, now pointed at relativity and the Standard Model instead. The user already went through non-relativistic quantum mechanics wanting the real formalism over pop-science metaphors ("spacetime is a rubber sheet," "everything is made of tiny billiard balls") and the same standard applies here.

## Success looks like
- Derive time dilation and length contraction from the two postulates of special relativity (not just quote the formulas)
- Explain what a quantum field is precisely enough to say why "the universe is made of particles" is the wrong picture — particles are excitations of fields
- State what the Einstein field equation says gravity actually is (spacetime curvature, not a force) at a conceptual level, having seen the equation itself even without solving it
- Describe the Standard Model's fermions, bosons, and three of the four fundamental forces (excluding gravity) accurately enough to say what's established physics vs. popular oversimplification

## Constraints
- No prior physics background beyond general familiarity, but very strong technical/CS background (linear algebra, formal notation comfortable) — same profile as [[../quantum-mechanics/MISSION.md|Quantum Mechanics]] and [[../quantum-computing/MISSION.md|Quantum Computing]]
- Builds on the Quantum Mechanics course already in progress (state vectors, Born rule) where the quantum-field material needs it, rather than re-deriving from scratch
- Self-study with an AI teacher

## Out of scope
- Non-relativistic quantum mechanics itself — that's its own course already
- The full computational machinery of QFT (path integrals, renormalization) or GR (tensor calculus, solving the field equations) — conceptual accuracy over doing the calculations
- Picking a side in open physics debates (string theory, quantum gravity candidates) — presenting the live state of the field accurately is the goal
