The Einstein Field Equation & What "Gravity is Geometry" Means

You have seen the picture: a bowling ball on a trampoline, marbles rolling in toward it. Lesson 1 called it rough, and lesson 4 replaced it with the real idea: free fall is force-free motion along geodesics of curved spacetime. This lesson shows the equation that decides how spacetime curves and reads it symbol by symbol, with no tensor calculus. Then you get two results you already trust back out of it: Newton’s gravity, and lesson 4’s 45.7 μs/day GPS offset. By the end you will know exactly what is wrong with the trampoline.

The metric: the interval, promoted to a field

Flat spacetime gives every pair of nearby events an interval that all observers agree on. General relativity keeps that idea and lets the coefficients vary from event to event. The metric tensor g_μν is a symmetric 4×4 matrix attached to every event, and it turns a small coordinate step dx into an interval:

ds² = g_μν dx^μ dx^ν  =  dxᵀ g dx        μ,ν = 0..3,  dx⁰ = c dt

Flat spacetime (lesson 2), the same at every event:
  η = diag(1, −1, −1, −1)     ds² = c²dt² − dx² − dy² − dz²

This uses the summation convention: an index appearing once up and once down is summed over 0 to 3, which makes g_μν dx^μ dx^ν exactly the quadratic form dxᵀ g dx. Symmetry leaves 10 independent entries per event. In CS terms, geometry is a matrix-valued function g : ℝ⁴ → Sym(4); flat spacetime is the constant η, and gravity is every other case.

Two links to lesson 4. The equivalence principle becomes a fact about g: at any one event, freely falling coordinates make g = η with zero first derivatives, so spacetime always looks flat locally. Only the second derivatives can’t be removed, and they are the curvature (Carroll’s notes, §2, count 20 per event). And ds² = 0 picks out the light cone at each event, so where g varies the cones tilt and narrow, as lesson 4 described.

The equation itself

G_μν + Λ g_μν = (8πG/c⁴) T_μν

G_μν = R_μν − ½R g_μν     Einstein tensor (curvature)
Λ                         cosmological constant
κ = 8πG/c⁴ ≈ 2.08×10⁻⁴³ N⁻¹
T_μν                      stress-energy tensor (matter)

(Signs as in Wikipedia and Carroll; in this course’s
 signature the Λ term flips: see the box below.)

The left side is geometry. R_μν is the Ricci curvature, R is its trace, and the Einstein tensor G_μν is built only from the metric and its first and second derivatives (Wikipedia). The right side is matter and energy. Both sides are symmetric 4×4 matrices, so this is really 10 coupled, nonlinear partial differential equations, and the unknown they solve for is the metric. Lesson 4’s Wheeler slogan is now exact. This equation is “matter tells spacetime how to curve,” and geodesic motion in the resulting metric is “spacetime tells matter how to move.”

The constant κ measures how stiff spacetime is. Its units, m/J, turn an energy density (J/m³) into a curvature (m⁻²). For water, ρc² = 9×10¹⁹ J/m³, so κρc² ≈ 1.9×10⁻²³ m⁻². That is a curvature scale of about 2×10¹¹ m, longer than the Earth–Sun distance. Everyday matter barely curves spacetime; it takes a planet or a star for the effect to add up.

Sign conventions Wikipedia, Carroll and Misner–Thorne–Wheeler use the signature (−,+,+,+), where flat spacetime is diag(−1,1,1,1) and g₀₀ ≈ −(1 + 2Φ/c²). This course keeps lesson 2’s (+,−,−,−), which flips the overall sign of g_μν. As a result the Λ term changes sign, and here the equation reads G_μν − Λg_μν = κT_μν (Wikipedia, “Sign convention”). No prediction changes. In both conventions, positive Λ means positive vacuum energy and accelerating expansion.

The right side: what actually gravitates

The stress-energy tensor T_μν describes the density and flow of four-momentum. Each entry T_αβ is the amount of α-momentum flowing in the β direction, where the 0-component of momentum is energy (Baez & Bunn, §3). Every entry is measured in J/m³:

T₀₀        energy density (ρc² for ordinary matter)
T₀ᵢ = Tᵢ₀  energy flux ÷ c  =  momentum density × c
Tᵢᵢ        pressure in direction i
Tᵢⱼ (i≠j)  shear stress
Fluid at rest:  T = diag(ρc², P, P, P)

In a planet, T₀₀ = ρc² is lesson 3’s rest energy per unit volume, and it outweighs every other entry. Still, the source of gravity is all ten independent entries, not just “mass.” Sunlight has no rest mass, but it carries energy density, energy flux and pressure, so it gravitates. Pressure gravitates too, and it adds to the pull.

The whole equation in one sentence

Baez and Bunn show that the full field equation can be said in one sentence (§3), with one piece of fine print: the sentence must hold for balls starting at rest in every local inertial frame, not just one. Take a small ball of freely falling test particles that start at rest relative to each other, and let V be its volume. At the first instant, ignoring Λ:

V̈/V = −4πG ( ρ_m + (Pₓ + Pᵧ + P_z)/c² )

Positive density and pressure make the ball shrink, which is to say gravity attracts. For ordinary matter the pressure term is negligible. At the center of Earth’s core, where P ≈ 360 GPa (Wikipedia, “Earth”) and ρ ≈ 13,000 kg/m³ (Wikipedia, “Earth’s inner core”), 3P/c² ≈ 1.2×10⁻⁵ kg/m³, which is about 10⁻⁹ of ρ. Inside a neutron star the two terms are comparable. There, the gravity of pressure helps drive heavy stars into collapse (§6). To get Newton out of the sentence, you only need one fact from linear algebra:

Each particle accelerates at a = −∇Φ. For two particles ξ apart:
  ξ̈ ≈ −H ξ,   H = Hessian of Φ  (Hᵢⱼ = ∂ᵢ∂ⱼΦ, symmetric 3×3)
Along H’s eigenvectors: ξₖ(t) ≈ ξₖ(0)(1 − ½hₖt²), so
  V ∝ ξ₁ξ₂ξ₃ ≈ V₀(1 − ½ tr(H) t²)   ⇒   V̈/V = −tr H = −∇²Φ
Match Baez–Bunn (no pressure):   ∇²Φ = 4πGρ    (Newton, as Poisson’s equation)

Outside a mass M (radial, sideways, sideways):
  H = (GM/r³)·diag(−2, +1, +1)     tr H = 0

Outside the mass, where ρ = 0, Poisson’s equation is solved by lesson 4’s Φ = −GM/r, the inverse-square law (Baez & Bunn get it straight from the ball). The last line of the block shows curvature in empty space. The trace is zero, so the ball keeps its volume (V̈ = 0, as T_μν = 0 requires). But the matrix is not zero, so the ball stretches toward the mass and squeezes sideways: lesson 4’s tidal effect as a traceless matrix (§5). In GR terms, the trace is a component of the Ricci curvature (R₀₀), which the field equation ties to T_μν point by point. The traceless part is the Weyl curvature, the only curvature in vacuum and what gravitational waves are made of. No matter here does not mean flat here.

Getting Newton and lesson 4 back out

The Newtonian limit has three conditions: a weak field (|Φ|/c² ≪ 1), slow motion (v ≪ c), and nothing changing in time. There, the 00 component of the field equation reduces to the ∇²Φ = 4πGρ you just derived, and the solution around a planet or star is this metric, in this course’s signs (Carroll eqs. 4.51, 6.29):

c²dτ² ≈ (1 + 2Φ/c²) c²dt² − (1 − 2Φ/c²)(dx² + dy² + dz²)

Clock at rest:  dτ/dt = √(1 + 2Φ/c²) ≈ 1 + Φ/c²      (√(1+ε) ≈ 1 + ε/2)
Two clocks:     Δτ/τ ≈ (Φ_high − Φ_low)/c² = ΔΦ/c²    (lesson 4)
Worked example: the GPS number, from g₀₀ 
Φ = −GM/r,   GM = 3.986×10¹⁴ m³/s²,   c² = 9×10¹⁶

           r (m)        Φ/c²          g₀₀ − 1
ground     6.371×10⁶   −6.95×10⁻¹⁰   −1.39×10⁻⁹
GPS        2.657×10⁷   −1.67×10⁻¹⁰   −3.33×10⁻¹⁰

ΔΦ/c² ≈ 5.28×10⁻¹⁰ × 86,400 s ≈ 45.7 μs/day   (orbit clock fast)
That is exactly lesson 4’s gravitational term. The elevator argument and the field equation give the same answer, and the whole effect comes from one matrix entry that differs from 1 by about one part in a billion.

Notice that only g₀₀ appeared in that calculation, and Carroll shows the same holds for every slow object. In ds², the spatial terms are multiplied by v²/c² compared with the time term. Even for Earth’s 30 km/s orbit around the Sun, v²/c² ≈ 10⁻⁸, so curved space hardly affects it. Apples and planets fall mainly because time runs at different rates in different places. That is precisely what the trampoline gets wrong: it uses gravity to explain gravity (something pulls the ball into the sheet), and it shows only curved space, the part that barely matters for slow objects. Light is the exception, since for light the spatial part counts as much as the time part. So GR predicts 1.75″ of starlight bending at the Sun’s edge, twice what curved time alone gives, with curved space supplying the other half (Will 2014, §4.1.1).

What the equation guarantees, and what it leaves open

Local energy-momentum conservation is built in. For any metric, ∇_μG^μν = 0 (the Bianchi identity; ∇_μ is the curved-spacetime divergence), and the metric’s divergence is zero too. So taking the divergence of both sides forces ∇_μT^μν = 0: four equations saying energy and momentum never appear or vanish at a point, they only flow. Up to the Λ term, G_μν is the only curvature with this property (Lovelock’s theorem), and Einstein’s earlier guess R_μν = κT_μν failed exactly this test (Carroll ch. 4). The conservation is local. Why an expanding universe has no general global energy law is lesson 8’s topic.

The cosmological constant Λ is the one extra term conservation allows, since g_μν has zero divergence. In the ball picture it acts like a uniform energy density in empty space whose pressure is minus that density. Pressure counts three times, so ρ + 3P/c² = −2ρ_Λ: balls grow, and cosmic expansion accelerates (§8). The measured value is about 10⁻⁵² m⁻², equal to a mass density of about 6×10⁻²⁷ kg/m³, or three or four hydrogen atoms per cubic metre (Wikipedia).

Established vs open, as of 2026 Tested: Mercury’s extra 43″/century of perihelion advance; light bending, confirmed to 0.01%; the Shapiro radar delay, confirmed by Cassini to about 1 part in 10⁵; gravitational redshift, from Pound–Rebka (1960) to GPS (Will 2014). Gravitational waves: GW150914 (peak strain 1.0×10⁻²¹) matched GR’s waveform for merging black holes (LIGO/Virgo 2016; Nobel 2017).
Open: what Λ is. Naive estimates of quantum vacuum energy (lesson 6) overshoot it by about 120 orders of magnitude (Carroll 2001). Whether Λ is even constant is also open: DESI’s second data release (2025) prefers a slowly changing dark energy at 2.8–4.2σ, depending on which supernova sample is combined (DESI DR2). As of 2026 that is a strong hint, short of the 5σ discovery standard. GR is classical, and no quantum theory of gravity has been confirmed (lesson 7). This lesson also never solved the equation. Black holes and cosmological models need the tensor machinery this course leaves out.

Practice before next lesson

Work through this by hand for the International Space Station, at an altitude of about 400 km (r = 6.771×10⁶ m), using Earth’s numbers above.

  1. Compute g₀₀ − 1 on the ground and at the ISS.
  2. From dτ/dt ≈ 1 + Φ/c², find how many μs per day the ISS clock gains from gravity.
  3. Find the slowdown from v = √(GM/r) and v²/2c², as in lesson 4’s GPS example.
  4. Net the two results.

You should find that the ISS clock loses about 25 μs/day, which is the opposite sign from GPS. Bonus: show that the two effects cancel for a circular orbit at r = 1.5 R_⊕.

Primary resource

Read John Baez & Emory Bunn, “The Meaning of Einstein’s Equation” (Am. J. Phys. 73, 644, 2005; free, also on arXiv). It states the whole equation as the ball-of-particles sentence, then uses that sentence to derive the inverse-square law, the gravity of pressure, tidal distortion and Λ, all without tensors. For the machinery this lesson stepped around, see ch. 4 of Carroll’s lecture notes or Susskind’s General Relativity course from lesson 4.

Ask your teacher anything. Try asking Claude Code: “Why is the ball’s volume change the trace of the tidal matrix?” (Jacobi’s formula for the derivative of a determinant; it makes the Ricci/Weyl split click.) Or ask why Einstein’s first guess, R_μν = κT_μν, breaks local energy conservation.
