Core Philosophy Cheat Sheet

The compressed vocabulary of the core branches — what each term means, and where it was taught. Every entry links back to the lesson holding the full explanation.

Triage: three kinds of question

KindSettled by — and the giveaway
EmpiricalObservation. Go and look; arguing harder is wasted effort.
ConceptualAnalysis. All facts agreed and the dispute survives — it is about what a concept requires.
NormativeArgument from values. All facts agreed and it still asks what ought to be.

Epistemology

TermDefinition
JTBKnowledge = justified true belief. Each condition necessary; jointly not sufficient.
Gettier caseJustified true belief that is true only by luck — the justification is not connected to what makes it true.
ReliabilismRepair: the belief must be produced by a reliable, truth-conducive process.
Safety / sensitivityModal repairs: still true in nearby worlds / would not be believed if false.
A priori / a posterioriJustified independently of experience / dependent on it. Rationalism vs empiricism splits on whether the first yields substantive truths.

Ethics

PositionWhat makes an act right
ConsequentialismIts outcomes are at least as good as any alternative's. Strains on the transplant surgeon.
Rule consequentialismIt follows the rule whose general adoption produces the best outcomes.
DeontologyIt violates no constraint — e.g. never treat a person merely as a means. Outcome-independent.
Virtue ethicsIt is what a person of good character would do. Arete (virtue), phronesis (practical wisdom), eudaimonia (flourishing).
MetaethicsSeparate layer: not which acts are right, but what kind of claim "X is wrong" even is.

Metaphysics

TermDefinition
Free will triad(1) Determinism, (2) freedom requires alternatives, (3) we are free. Inconsistent — every position drops one.
Hard determinismDrops (3). No free will, no desert.
LibertarianismDrops (1). Owes an account of how undetermined events are yours.
CompatibilismDrops (2). Freedom = acting from your own unobstructed reasons. Dominant position.
Frankfurt caseResponsible though unable to do otherwise — the counterexample that kills (2).
Psychological continuityLockean criterion of personal identity. Broken by branching: two continuers, one identity.

Method — the five moves

MoveWhat it does
CounterexampleRestate the thesis as a universal, find one coherent case where it fails. Coherent is enough — the thesis claims necessity.
Thought experimentStipulate away everything but one variable. Challenge it on coherence, intuition stability, or question-begging.
CharityReconstruct the opponent at full strength first. Its failure mode is the straw man.
ReductioAssume, derive absurdity, reject. Escape hatch: bite the bullet.
Reflective equilibriumMutually adjust principles and case verdicts until they cohere. Revise whichever has weaker support.

Movements — existentialism, mind, Stoicism

TermDefinition
Existence precedes essenceNo pre-given human nature; what you are is the running total of what you do.
Bad faithSelf-deception that recasts a choice as a fact about your role or nature.
The absurdA relation: our demand for meaning colliding with a silent universe. Camus' response is revolt.
AuthenticityOwning a choice as yours. Not "be your true self" — that smuggles back an essence.
Hard problemWhy is any functional process accompanied by experience at all? Distinct from the "easy" mechanism questions.
QualiaThe felt, subjective qualities of experience — what function seems to leave out.
Knowledge argumentMary knows all physical facts, learns something on release, so not all facts are physical. Replies deny P2.
Zombie argumentPhysical duplicate without experience. Contested step: conceivable ⟹ possible.
FunctionalismA mental state is its causal role — hence multiply realizable in any substrate.
Virtue is the only goodStoic core claim. Everything external is indifferent to whether life goes well.
Preferred indifferentsHealth, wealth, reputation — genuinely valuable, energetically pursued, yet not constituents of the good life.
Up to usAssent to impressions, and essentially nothing else. Narrower than "what you control."
Passions as judgmentsAn emotion contains a judgment, so it can be false — and corrected rather than suppressed.

Thinkers — one move each

ThinkerThe move
PlatoKnowledge is not perception — it is of the abstract, not the particular.
AristotleForm is in the thing. Invented logic, and virtue ethics with it.
DescartesStart from doubt; rebuild on foundations. Bequeathed the mind-body problem.
HumeFollow empiricism all the way down — causation, induction and the self all take damage. Is-ought gap.
KantAsk what experience presupposes. The transcendental turn; the categorical imperative.
MillMake the moral calculus defensible and public. The harm principle.
NietzscheGenealogy — ask where a value came from, not whether it is true.
WittgensteinSuspect the question. Meaning as picture, then meaning as use.
FregeQuantifiers and variables — ended the syllogism's monopoly.
