Robot Kinematics Cheat Sheet

Joints, links, DOF, and the Go2's real joint layout — compressed from Lesson 3.

Vocabulary

TermDefinition
LinkA rigid segment of the robot's body — doesn't bend.
JointA connection between two links that allows relative motion. Every Go2 joint is revolute (rotates around one fixed axis).
Degree of freedom (DOF)One independent way something can move. One revolute joint = one rotational DOF.
Kinematic chainA sequence of links connected by joints, one after another — each Go2 leg is a 3-joint chain.
Floating baseThe 6 unactuated DOF (3 translation + 3 rotation) any free rigid body has in 3D — here, the robot's own trunk.
Forward kinematicsJoint angles → end-effector (foot) position. The easy direction.
Inverse kinematicsEnd-effector position → joint angles. The hard direction — not covered in Lesson 3.

Thigh and calf share a rotation axis, so a single 2D side view can show both. Hip rotates around a different axis and cannot be shown correctly in that same flat view.

DOF accounting

Per leg3 joints × 1 DOF each = 3 DOF
All 4 legs4 × 3 = 12 actuated DOF
Floating base (the trunk itself)6 unactuated DOF (3 translation + 3 rotation) — not driven by any motor
Whole robot18 total DOF, 12 commandable

Worth remembering This exact mapping already caused one real mistake in this curriculum — the PD control lesson originally mislabeled index 6 as "front-right" when it's actually rear-right. Double-check against go2.xml before trusting any index-to-leg claim, including this table.
