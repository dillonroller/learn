Sim-to-Real Gap Cheat Sheet

The compressed version of lesson 7: what a simulator approximates, what to distrust from a sim-only run, and the source project's own verification framing.

What MuJoCo approximates (not replicates)

CategoryReal-world difference
Friction & contactSurface, wear, moisture — one chosen contact model vs. reality
Actuator dynamicsTorque/rate limits, response delay, heat buildup under load
Sensor noiseBias drift, vibration coupling — real IMU/encoder noise has its own statistics
LatencyReal network/DDS and control-loop jitter
PowerBattery voltage sag under load, changing available torque over time
Mechanical wearCable drag, gearbox backlash, joint play — accumulate only on real hardware

This project's verification stages

StageAnswers
Simulation (unitree_mujoco)Does the logic/control law behave as intended at all?
Controlled real-hardware trialDoes it survive contact with real actuators, sensors, and physics?
Field trialDoes it hold up in the real deployment environment (crowds, surfaces, network)?

A feasibility rating (source project's architecture doc, Layer 6) is only as strong as the weakest stage actually completed — "Ready with existing robot" implies more than a clean sim run. stand_up has cleared stage one only: it now reaches mission_completed in unitree_mujoco (≈0.028 rad error, reproducible across repeated runs — see run_stand_mission.py's tuning-history docstring). Stages two and three haven't happened yet.

Quotable framing (the source project's own words)

"How do we prove readiness before trusting the robot in production?" — architecture doc, Layer 5
"...lets a feasibility report cite evidence instead of assertion: not 'the robot can probably do this' but 'in N simulated runs and M field trials, these specific events fired in these specific sequences...'" — telemetry-contract doc

Quick test: does this claim need real hardware?

Ask: does the claim depend on anything in the table above (friction, actuator limits, sensor noise, latency, power, wear) or on the real deployment environment (crowds, surfaces, network)? If yes, a sim run alone cannot settle it.

Primary source Zhao, Peña Queralta & Westerlund, "Sim-to-Real Transfer in Deep Reinforcement Learning for Robotics: a Survey" (arXiv:2009.13303)
